================================================================================
GOAL
================================================================================

Build a sequence-modeling architecture that is

  (i)  at least as capable as a modern Transformer LLM on real language-modeling
       and reasoning benchmarks at iso-FLOPs, AND

  (ii) more efficient — strictly lower training and/or inference cost at iso-
       quality, on real hardware.

A "win" requires both (i) and (ii) on real benchmarks. Toy synthetic results
(needle, A_5 word-recognition, HMM hidden-state, MQAR-synthetic) are *gates*
that show a candidate mechanism is not architecturally broken. They are NOT
the goal. The goal is to ship something that could replace Mamba-2 / Llama /
Mixtral in a real deployment.

================================================================================
WHAT WE ALREADY HAVE (Phase 1)
================================================================================

Phase 1 (VESTA, May 2026) produced a structurally novel mechanism and a
positive synthetic-task result. Treat it as a PASSED GATE, not as the goal.

  - Selected framework: GRP-RNN (Group-Rotation Product Recurrent Network).
    Per-step transition: R_t = ∏_{k=1..K} G_k(θ_k(x_t)) ∈ SO(m). With K=m
    interlocking-plane Givens rotations and input-dependent angles, the
    reachable transition group is non-abelian and contains A_5, escaping the
    Merrill/Petty/Sabharwal 2024 diagonal-SSM expressivity bound.
    See `research/VESTA_FRAMEWORK.md`.

  - Empirical gate-pass on A_5 word recognition (synthetic, single-layer m=256
    K=256): val_acc 1.00 across multi-seed at T ∈ {8, 32, 64, 128} and 0.99
    at T=256, with the LRU-equivalent baseline (diagonal SSM, with or without
    input-dep phase, with LayerNorm) stuck at random across all T.
    See `research/VESTA_REPORT.md` and `research/vesta/MITIGATIONS_RESULTS.md`.

  - Required mitigations: per-step LayerNorm + multi-phase curriculum for
    T ≥ 64. LayerNorm alone is sufficient at T ≤ 32.

  - C++ implementation: `research/ealrmn_gpu/model_grp_rnn.cuh` (~440 LOC) +
    `main.cu` extensions + `tasks.cuh::A5WordTask`. Gradcheck PASS.

What Phase 1 did NOT do:
  - Did not test on natural-language data.
  - Did not test multi-layer stacking.
  - Did not benchmark throughput against Mamba-2.
  - Did not measure perplexity at any scale.
  - Did not run the framework on any standard LM eval suite.

The Phase 1 result is "a single-layer linear-recurrence architecture has
expressivity Mamba-class diagonal SSMs provably lack on a synthetic task."
This is an interesting building block. It is not LLM replacement.

================================================================================
PHASE 2 — WHAT IT TAKES TO MOVE TOWARD THE GOAL
================================================================================

Phase 2 is the program that decides whether the Phase 1 architecture matters
for natural language. Pass-criteria are graded; the gates must be cleared in
sequence.

GATE-2A  Per-token LM readout (infrastructure, not a research result)
GATE-2B  Multi-layer stacking on a real corpus
GATE-2C  Match Mamba-2-equivalent at small scale (~10M-100M params)
GATE-2D  Beat Mamba-2-equivalent on at least one axis (recall, reasoning,
         or efficiency) at the same small scale
GATE-2E  Demonstrate the advantage holds at a 4-10× param scale-up
GATE-2F  Demonstrate inference-time efficiency vs a production Transformer

Each gate has a pre-committed pass/fail threshold (see §Falsifiable claims).
Failing a gate kills the program at that gate and the result is published as
negative. Skipping ahead is forbidden.

================================================================================
HARD PROHIBITIONS (lessons from prior programs)
================================================================================

P1. Do not propose new mechanisms before a literature audit of the closest
    existing methods. Phase 1's failure mode was that EALRMN invented
    KOSM+RDPC+SSVIB before checking that linear recurrence + orthogonal init
    already gave most of the win. Audit-first is non-negotiable.

P2. Do not test on synthetic-only tasks and call it an LM result. Synthetic
    tasks are gates. The decisive evals are: (a) pretraining perplexity on a
    real corpus slice, (b) downstream-eval accuracy (LAMBADA, HellaSwag,
    PIQA, ARC, etc.), (c) reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K subset, MMLU subset),
    (d) recall benchmarks (Zoology MQAR variants on real Pile slices).
    Anything reported without (a) or (b) is a *gate result*, not a *goal
    result*.

P3. Do not stack new mechanisms without iso-flag ablation. Every component
    must be ablatable in code (single flag) and tested in isolation against
    the strongest existing baseline for the axis it improves. Phase 1's
    architecture has six flags; preserve this discipline.

P4. Do not report wall-clock results without a same-hardware Mamba-2 (or
    Llama / Mixtral) baseline measured under identical conditions. Brief-
    cited Mamba-2 throughput numbers are from H100 / specific PyTorch
    versions and are not directly comparable to RTX 4080 SUPER runs.

P5. Do not pass a gate on a single seed. Multi-seed (>= 3 minimum; >= 5
    preferred) is required for any claimed win. Report per-seed numbers,
    median, and worst case. A 4/5-seed pass with one seed failing is itself
    informative; report it.

P6. Do not silently drop a falsified claim. If Phase 2 falsifies the Phase 1
    expressivity advantage at scale, write that down and publish it.
    Phase 1's tanh-vs-linear ablation that retroactively invalidated EALRMN's
    "magnitudes" claim is the precedent.

P7. Do not pursue Phase-1-style architecture inventions until Phase 2 has
    cleared Gate-2C or higher. Phase 2's job is to validate or kill the
    *existing* Phase-1 architecture before any new mechanism is added.

================================================================================
PHASE 2 PROGRAM (gates, in order)
================================================================================

GATE-2A — Per-token LM readout
------------------------------

Currently `model_grp_rnn.cuh` produces a single classification head reading
`s_T → logits`. To do LM, the readout must produce `logits_t = W_out @ s_t`
for every t, and the loss must be the sum of per-token cross-entropies.

Implementation (~150 LOC):
  - Extend `GRPRNNModelCache` with `logits_all: (T, B, V)` tensor.
  - Extend `forward()` to compute logits at each step.
  - New `compute_lm_loss()` summing per-token cross-entropies.
  - New `backward_lm()` propagating per-token gradients into the recurrence
    at every step (not just at t=T).
  - Gradcheck the LM forward+backward at small scale (B=2, T=8, m=8).

Gate-2A is PASSED when:
  - LM forward+backward gradcheck 30+/30+ PASS on the existing infrastructure.
  - Plain tanh-RNN trained with `--task=syntheticlm` for small steps shows
    measurable val_loss reduction below random (log V) — sanity that the
    LM training loop works.

Failing Gate-2A means the LM infrastructure is broken; debug before continuing.

GATE-2B — Multi-layer stacking
------------------------------

A single-layer GRP-RNN+LN cannot beat Transformer LMs even on small corpora.
LLMs are deep. Implementation:

  - Stack L ∈ {2, 4, 8} GRP-RNN+LN blocks with residual connections.
  - Add a tokenizer or use byte-level inputs (V=256).
  - Standard pre-norm-residual structure: x_l = x_{l-1} + GRP-RNN-Block(x_{l-1}).
  - FFN / SwiGLU MLP between recurrence blocks if it helps (ablate explicitly).

Gate-2B is PASSED when:
  - Multi-layer GRP-RNN+LN trains to lower val_loss than a same-flop
    single-layer GRP-RNN+LN on a real-corpus slice. Specifically: train on
    ~50M-100M tokens of byte-level WikiText or OpenWebText slice with
    AdamW, m ∈ {128, 256, 512}, L ∈ {2, 4}, batch=8 T=2048 for ~10K steps.
    Multi-layer should beat single-layer by ≥0.1 nat val_loss.
  - Multi-seed (≥3) confirms.

Failing Gate-2B means the GRP-RNN+LN block does not benefit from stacking on
real data; the architecture is research-grade only.

GATE-2C — Match Mamba-2-equivalent at small scale
-------------------------------------------------

The strong baseline. Two options:

  Option C1 (preferred, cleaner): Reproduce a small Mamba-2 (or a published
  reproducer) on the same hardware and same data slice, at iso-FLOPs and
  iso-params (~10M-100M total params, ~1-4B training tokens). Measure
  perplexity, throughput, and one downstream eval (LAMBADA accuracy).

  Option C2 (cheaper fallback): Use the published Mamba-2 throughput +
  perplexity numbers at the same param/token budget, adjusted for hardware
  ratio. This is weaker — direct same-hardware comparison is preferred.

Gate-2C is PASSED when:
  - GRP-RNN+LN multi-layer val_loss / perplexity within ±10% of Mamba-2-
    equivalent at iso-FLOPs and iso-params.
  - LAMBADA-style downstream eval within ±10% of the Mamba-2 baseline.

Failing Gate-2C means the Phase-1 expressivity advantage does not survive
real-LM training, and the program publishes a negative result and stops
(unless an explicit pre-committed mitigation is tried). This is the most
likely program-killing gate.

GATE-2D — Beat Mamba-2-equivalent on at least one axis
-----------------------------------------------------

If Gate-2C passes (we MATCH Mamba-2), Gate-2D asks: do we BEAT it on any
axis? Three candidate axes, each with a pre-committed measurable threshold:

  (D-recall)  On a Zoology MQAR-real evaluation slice from Pile or similar,
              GRP-RNN+LN beats Mamba-2 by ≥0.05 accuracy at iso-params.
              Hypothesis: the non-diagonal expressivity helps multi-key
              recall.

  (D-reason)  On a small algebraic / reasoning eval (modular arithmetic,
              chain composition, dyck grammar), GRP-RNN+LN beats Mamba-2
              by ≥0.10 accuracy. Hypothesis: the A_5 expressivity advantage
              transfers to other state-tracking-like reasoning.

  (D-efficiency)  Training tok/s OR inference tok/s ≥1.5× Mamba-2 at iso-
                  quality. Hypothesis: simpler kernels + fewer parameters
                  yield wall-clock wins.

Gate-2D is PASSED when at least one of (D-recall, D-reason, D-efficiency)
clears its threshold with ≥3 seeds and 95% CI not crossing zero.

Failing Gate-2D means GRP-RNN+LN matches Mamba-2 but doesn't beat it; this
is publishable as a tie-result but not as LLM replacement progress.

GATE-2E — Scale-up demonstration
-------------------------------

Whatever axis won at Gate-2D, demonstrate it holds at 4-10× the param
budget. Specifically:

  - Train the winning configuration at 4× and 10× the Gate-2C params/tokens.
  - Report val_loss, throughput, and the winning-axis metric.
  - The advantage at Gate-2D must hold (within 20% relative) at both 4× and
    10× scale.

Failing Gate-2E means the advantage was small-scale-specific; report and
stop.

GATE-2F — Inference-time efficiency
----------------------------------

Final practical gate. The architecture must be deployable as a runtime
generator with competitive throughput.

  - Implement KV-cache-free generation (linear-recurrence is inherently this).
  - Benchmark tokens-per-second at batch=1 generation against a same-quality
    Transformer (Llama-3-1B or similar) on the same hardware.
  - Target: ≥2× faster than Transformer at iso-quality, ≥0.5× of Mamba-2.

Failing Gate-2F means the architecture is a research artifact, not a
deployment-grade LLM.

================================================================================
MANDATORY BASELINE MATRIX (per gate)
================================================================================

Every gate report MUST include numbers from these baselines on the same
data, same hardware, same training budget:

  B1. GRP-RNN+LN (the proposal under test)
  B2. LRU + LN, multi-layer (the diagonal-SSM control)
  B3. Mamba-2 reference (when available; the audit's strongest pure
      recurrence)
  B4. Small causal Transformer at iso-params (the gold-standard reference)
  B5. Vanilla linear-RNN + orthogonal init (the Phase-1 B0 floor)

Reports without B2 and B4 are not gate-passable. B3 is preferred for any
gate ≥ 2C.

================================================================================
WHAT WE ARE NOT DOING IN PHASE 2
================================================================================

Out of scope unless GRP-RNN+LN clears Gate-2C:
  - New architectural mechanisms beyond GRP-RNN+LN (the parked Candidates B
    and C remain parked).
  - R5 (mixture-of-experts), R6 (memory-write regularization), R8 (optional
    decoding) explorations.
  - Multi-modal extensions.
  - Tokenizer research.
  - The 7-term Lagrangian that EALRMN tried (forbidden).

Out of scope unless GRP-RNN+LN clears Gate-2E:
  - Production deployment infrastructure.
  - Distributed training.
  - Custom hardware kernels beyond what existing glades-trainer already has.

================================================================================
HONEST FAILURE MODES PRE-REGISTERED
================================================================================

F-Phase2-1. Per-token LM gradients destabilize the K-Givens chain. Symptom:
            multi-layer GRP-RNN+LN diverges or stalls in LM training.
            Mitigation: gradient checkpoint at layer boundaries, smaller
            learning rate, or reduce K from m to m/4. If unfixable in
            2 days, declare Gate-2A fail and stop.

F-Phase2-2. GRP-RNN+LN matches but does not beat Mamba-2. Most likely
            outcome (median expectation). The architecture's expressivity
            advantage is real but doesn't manifest in natural-language
            perplexity. Report as a tie-result. The program then has two
            paths: (a) declare done and publish, (b) re-target to D-reason
            or D-efficiency where the gap might be larger.

F-Phase2-3. The advantage holds at small scale but vanishes at 4-10× param
            scale-up. Phase-0k of EALRMN observed this pattern; treat as
            the prior probability for any small-scale win. If realized,
            report and stop.

F-Phase2-4. Throughput is too slow due to sequential Givens application.
            The current K=m chain costs O(K·T) sequential ops per layer.
            At K=512 T=2048, that's 1M sequential ops per layer per
            forward — likely uncompetitive without kernel fusion.
            Mitigation: smaller K (m/4 or m/8), kernel fusion with input
            GEMV, or accept the slowdown and target D-recall / D-reason
            instead of D-efficiency.

F-Phase2-5. Multi-phase curriculum doesn't transfer to natural-language
            pretraining. The A_5 result needed curriculum at T ≥ 64.
            Natural-language pretraining at long T may need analogous
            curriculum; we don't yet know how to design it. Mitigation:
            start with short context (T=512), grow gradually, document
            the schedule that works (if any).

================================================================================
ITERATION DISCIPLINE
================================================================================

Each gate report includes:
  - Pre-registration (claim, threshold, baselines, seeds, budget).
  - The results table, all variants, all seeds.
  - The pre-commit interpretation table (SUPPORTED / FALSIFIED /
    INCONCLUSIVE).
  - The honest narrative: what we expected, what we observed, what changed.

Memory is updated after each gate:
  - Mark the gate as PASSED / FAILED / INCONCLUSIVE.
  - Save the regime in which the result holds.
  - Save the failure mode if applicable.

Between gates, the framework document (`research/VESTA_FRAMEWORK.md`) and
the program report (`research/VESTA_REPORT.md`) are updated. The brief
itself (this file) is updated only at major program inflections.

================================================================================
STYLE
================================================================================

- The goal is LLM replacement. Synthetic-task wins are evidence, not the
  product.
- Be mathematically precise on architecture; be empirically honest on
  evaluation.
- Pre-commit baselines and thresholds before running.
- Multi-seed every claimed win.
- Compare against Mamba-2 / Transformer at iso-FLOPs and iso-params on real
  data. Anything else is a synthetic-task gate.
- When a gate fails, write it down and publish the negative. Do not silently
  re-target.

================================================================================
USE THE EXISTING `research-framework-design` AND `claude-api` SKILLS
================================================================================

Phase 1 used the `research-framework-design` skill for the inventive step.
Phase 2 is mostly EXECUTION (per-token readout, multi-layer stacking, real
training, baseline comparisons) — not invention. The `research-framework-
design` skill is appropriate ONLY for designing the per-token-LM-loss term,
the multi-layer stacking pattern, and the eval suite. For the rest, prefer
straightforward engineering.

If a Phase 2 gate fails AND the program has explicit pre-committed
permission to extend the architecture, then `research-framework-design`
applies for designing the targeted extension. Otherwise stick to execution.

================================================================================
APPENDIX: WHY PHASE 1's A_5 RESULT IS NOT ENOUGH ON ITS OWN
================================================================================

The A_5 word-recognition result demonstrates a *theoretical expressivity
gap* empirically. It does NOT demonstrate:
  - That the gap matters for natural language. Most natural-language tokens
    are not produced by non-solvable-group state-tracking; they are produced
    by approximately-Markov surface processes that diagonal SSMs already
    handle well.
  - That GRP-RNN scales. We have only single-layer m=256 results.
  - That GRP-RNN is efficient. We have only synthetic-task throughput
    numbers, and at K=m it is 5× slower than vanilla linear RNN.
  - That GRP-RNN's multi-phase curriculum has a natural-language analogue.

Phase 2 exists to convert the Phase-1 expressivity-gap demonstration into
either (a) a deployable LLM-replacement architecture or (b) a clean
negative result that says "the A_5 advantage does not transfer to natural
language at the scales tested." Both outcomes are publishable. Only one is
LLM replacement.
