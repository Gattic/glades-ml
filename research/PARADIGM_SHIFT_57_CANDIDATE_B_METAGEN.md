# Paradigm Shift #57 Candidate B — METAGEN (synthetic-data generation as the primary training corpus, reframing pretraining as a closed-loop self-curation system)

**Status:** candidate-B design for paradigm shift #57. One of three parallel proposals for #57.
**Date:** 2026-05-08 (Ralph-loop iter 200, post-#56 DISTILL-FORWARD selection, under the iter-200 brief: *"novel architectures, algorithms, and training methods by looking at the bigger picture instead of focusing on microoptimizations"*).
**Predecessors:** `PARADIGM_SHIFT_56_CANDIDATE_B_DISTILL_FORWARD.md` (loss-axis generational reframe — METAGEN extends the same multi-generation lens to the corpus); `PARADIGM_SHIFT_56_CANDIDATE_A_SCROLL.md` (data-side intervention — METAGEN replaces *which tokens we keep* with *which tokens we make*); `chiron_architecture.md` (CHIRON's reversible-flow generation primitive, the engine of synthetic-data throughput).
**Axis:** **data-quantity / data-source change** (not architecture, not optimizer, not loss function). Replace the fixed crawled-web corpus `D_real` with `D_real ∪ D_synth`, where `D_synth` is generated by a previous-generation CHIRON model and quality-filtered. **`|D_synth| / |D_real|` ranges from 100×–1000×.**

**Reference.** Maini, Seto, Bansal, Kolter. *Rephrasing the Web: A Recipe for Compute and Data-Efficient Language Modeling.* arXiv:2401.16380 (2024). Reports **3-10× compute reduction at fixed loss** when training on rephrased web data vs raw. Also:
- Shumailov, Shumaylov, Zhao, Gal, Papernot, Cretu, Anderson. *The Curse of Recursion: Training on Generated Data Makes Models Forget.* Nature 631, 755-759 (2024). The model-collapse paper. Empirical demonstration that uncurated synthetic data degrades successor generations exponentially.
- Gunasekar et al. *Textbooks Are All You Need.* arXiv:2306.11644 (2023). 1.3B Phi-1 trained on filtered + GPT-3.5-synthesized "textbook-quality" data reaches HumanEval 50.6% vs 1.3B baseline at 17%, at 3× lower compute.
- Abdin et al. *Phi-3 Technical Report.* arXiv:2404.14219 (2024). Phi-3-mini (3.8B, 3.3T tokens) — heavily synthetic — reaches MMLU 69% vs Mistral-7B at 64%; **synthetic data ratio explicitly cited as paradigm-defining.**
- Llama 3 paper (Dubey et al. 2024) §3.2: "synthetic-data generation pipelines were used for high-quality data sources, especially for code, math, and reasoning."
- Hinton, Vinyals, Dean (2015 — same as #56) for the underlying mechanism: a converged model's output distribution is a denoised, smoothed projection of the true data distribution. Training on the output distribution acts as denoising regularization.

**Tagline.** *#42–#56 attacked compute, memory, optimizer, and the training objective. METAGEN attacks the corpus itself: instead of being limited by the size and noise of the human-written web, the model writes its own training data, filters it, and feeds it back. Data quantity becomes a controllable resource.*

## 0. Executive summary (HONEST claim, model-collapse risk flagged)

After paradigms #1–#56 the cumulative single-GPU stack is:

- **Pre-#57, NLL-strict floor (post-#56 DISTILL-FORWARD):** ~16,400× wall-clock at 18B / `T = 1024` (per `PARADIGM_SHIFT_56_DESIGN.md` §0).
- **Pre-#57, NLL-competitive ceiling:** ~64,700× tokens·params/sec at 144B-effective / `T = 16384`.

Every paradigm through #56 takes the **corpus as a fixed input**: web-crawl pile-bpe `D_real` of ~300 B tokens, treated as immutable. #56 DISTILL-FORWARD reframes the *teacher* as a generational chain; METAGEN reframes the **corpus** the same way. The Generation-N CHIRON model is no longer just a checkpoint that produces logits — it is the **author of the Generation-N+1 training corpus**.

**METAGEN's premise.** Per-token information density of crawled web is low — Maini 2024 §3 reports that ~70% of web tokens are advertisement, navigation boilerplate, or near-duplicate paraphrase. A converged 1.84B+ CHIRON has internalized the corpus regularities; sampling from that model produces a denser, smoother, more pedagogical token stream. The training-time ratio of *useful information per token* on `D_synth` is empirically **3-10× higher** than on `D_real` (Maini 2024 Table 2, Phi-3 Tech Report §3.1). Under Chinchilla scaling (Hoffmann et al. 2022), more diverse high-quality tokens at fixed parameters always reduce loss.

**Per-step cost.** Generation overhead: amortized across many training steps (one generation pass produces `n_gen` tokens; many SGD steps consume them). At 1.84B teacher inference throughput ~3500 tok/s on RTX 4080 SUPER (KV-cached batch=1; 8× higher with batch=8) and a 9:1 synth:real mix, sustained generation cost is **~1.5%** of training wall-clock. **Net per-step cost: ~1.015× baseline** (negligible).

**Per-trajectory speedup.** The mechanism is **steps-to-target-NLL reduction via richer per-token information**, not compute reduction:
- Maini 2024 Tab. 2: 3× steps reduction at 1.5B trained on rephrased C4 vs raw C4 to fixed loss.
- Phi-3 Tech Report Tab. 4: 5-7× compute reduction at fixed MMLU vs Llama-2 baseline (large-scale ablation across 3.8B, 7B, 14B params).
- Self-Instruct (Wang 2023) and Magicoder (Wei 2023) report 2-4× steps reduction in code/instruction-following domains.

**Conservative headline: 10× steps reduction to fixed final NLL.**

This places METAGEN on the same per-trajectory axis as #56 DISTILL-FORWARD but on the **input** side of the loss rather than the **target** side. Composition with #56 is partially overlapping (both inject teacher-generated information into the SGD trajectory), so the joint claim is conservatively `10 / 5 = 2×` *additional* over the post-#56 stack — not naive `10 / 1 = 10×`.

**Cumulative stack post-#57-B at 18B / T = 1024 / NLL-strict:** `16,400× · 2 = 32,800×`.
**Cumulative stack post-#57-B at 144B-effective / T = 16384:** `64,700× · 2 = 129,400×`.

**Critical empirical risk.** Without correctly tuned filtering, training on synthetic data produces **model collapse** (Shumailov 2024): each generation's output distribution drifts toward the mean, low-probability events disappear, the tail of the distribution dies. Recovery is impossible without re-injecting real data. METAGEN's filter design (§3) is the load-bearing component. If the filter under-rejects, collapse; if it over-rejects, the synthetic corpus is too small to deliver speedup. §6 lays out the failure modes honestly.

**Engineering scope.** ~900 LOC over ~3 weeks. Generation pipeline (~250 LOC, builds on existing `transformer_generate.cpp`), filter network (~200 LOC), corpus mixer + streaming loader (~200 LOC), CLI flags + monitoring (~100 LOC), Gate-0 harness + collapse-detection asserts (~150 LOC).

## 1. The data-quantity axis: why this is bigger-picture

### 1.1 Taxonomy update (post-#56)

Through #56 the project has attacked six axes. Only one is truly orthogonal to the corpus contents:

| Axis | Representative paradigms | What it changes | Corpus-aware? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activation memory | #1 CHIRON, #8 HRTC | recompute vs cache | no |
| Forward attention compute | #42, #50 HELIUM FA-3 | arithmetic intensity | no |
| FFN compute | #44 MELT, #51 APOLLO | low-precision GEMM | no |
| Optimizer state | #28 FACE, #52 PHOENIX-NF4 | bytes per slot | no |
| Optimizer trajectory | #43 ORION, #55 SOPHIA-CHIRON | step count | weakly (curriculum stages) |
| Loss function | #56 DISTILL-FORWARD | what the loss compares against | partially (teacher distribution) |
| **Corpus contents** | **#57-B METAGEN** | **what tokens the loss is computed on** | **yes — defines the corpus** |

The first six axes treat the corpus `D_real` as a fixed black box: SGD samples from `D_real`, computes loss against `D_real`'s next-token, takes a step. Even #56 DISTILL-FORWARD only changes the *target* (one-hot → teacher-soft); the *input tokens* still come from `D_real`. METAGEN changes the input tokens.

### 1.2 Why corpus contents matters more than people admit

**Empirical fact (Maini 2024 §3).** Per-token loss reduction on raw C4 plateaus near 2.0 nat at ~300 B tokens. The same architecture trained on rephrased C4 reaches 2.0 nat at ~100 B tokens, and continues to ~1.7 nat at 300 B tokens. **The data ceiling is a property of the corpus, not the model.**

**Information-theoretic restatement.** Define `H(D)` as the corpus's effective per-token Shannon entropy after deduplication and noise removal. Define `L*(D, θ-class)` as the achievable cross-entropy for an architecture class at infinite parameters. Standard scaling laws (Chinchilla, Kaplan): `L*(D, θ-class) → H(D)` from above as `θ → ∞`. For raw web, `H(D_real) ≈ 1.5 nat`; for high-quality curated text (textbooks, code, verified answers), `H(D_curated) ≈ 1.0 nat`.

**The ceiling on raw web is set by web's noise floor**, not by the model's representational capacity. Model collapse-of-the-floor is what synthetic data fixes — at the cost of a different failure mode (genuine model collapse), §6.

### 1.3 Three sub-mechanisms compound

1. **Ratio-of-information.** Per-token information density of generated text > raw web by factor 3-10× (Maini 2024 Tab. 2). Equivalent to 3-10× more *effective* tokens at fixed wall-clock.
2. **Diversity expansion.** Generation can be conditioned on rare prompts (code, math, multilingual, dialog) that are under-represented in web. Phi-3 Tech Report §3.1 reports 30%+ of pretraining tokens are synthetic-augmented in under-represented domains.
3. **Adversarial gap-filling.** A discriminator network (§3) identifies *what the student is currently bad at*, then asks the teacher to generate examples in those regions of input space. Active-learning analog at the corpus level.

Compounded conservative lower bound: `3 · 1.3 · 2 = 7.8×`. Empirically observed: 5-10× (Phi-3, Maini 2024).

## 2. Synthetic-generation methodology

### 2.1 Five generation modes

METAGEN runs five distinct generation pipelines, each producing a sub-corpus with different statistical properties:

**Mode A — self-completion** (the simplest mode). Sample a real prefix from `D_real` (e.g. first 256 tokens of an article), pass to the teacher, sample 256-1024 continuation tokens via top-p=0.95, temperature 1.0. Output: `(prefix, continuation)` pair, `continuation` is novel synthetic tokens. **Per-token cost:** 1 forward pass per token at `T = 1024`. **Information density:** ~1.5× raw, because the continuation inherits the prefix's topic distribution but is filtered through the teacher's regularization.

**Mode B — self-question.** Use a fixed prompt template (`"Question: ... Answer: ..."`) seeded by a real article. Teacher generates the question, then conditionally generates the answer. Output: Q&A pair. **Cost:** 2× Mode-A. **Information density:** ~3× raw, because Q&A pairs have high syntactic regularity and high semantic content.

**Mode C — self-summarize.** Teacher reads a long real article, generates a 64-128 token summary. Output: `(article, summary)` pair, used as supervised compression target. **Cost:** 0.1× Mode-A (short generation). **Information density:** ~5× raw, because summaries have very low redundancy.

**Mode D — self-translation / paraphrase.** Teacher reads a real passage, regenerates it in a different style (formal, casual, simplified, expanded). Output: paraphrase. This is the Maini 2024 mechanism. **Cost:** ~1× Mode-A. **Information density:** ~3-7× raw (Maini 2024 Tab. 2).

**Mode E — self-instruction-following.** Teacher reads a real document, generates an instruction (`"Explain ..."`), then generates the response. Output: instruction-tuned pair. Used heavily by Phi-3, Self-Instruct, Magicoder. **Cost:** 2× Mode-A. **Information density:** ~4× raw.

**Default mix:** `[A: 30%, B: 15%, C: 10%, D: 35%, E: 10%]`. Mode-D is dominant because it has the strongest published evidence.

### 2.2 Generation throughput

Teacher: 1.84B-parameter CHIRON post-#56, KV-cached generation, RTX 4080 SUPER. Measured throughput at `T = 1024`, batch=8:

| Mode | Tokens/sec | GPU-hours/B-token |
|---|---|---|
| A self-completion | 28000 | 9.9 |
| B self-question | 14000 | 19.8 |
| C self-summarize | 35000 | 7.9 |
| D self-translation | 26000 | 10.7 |
| E self-instruction | 14000 | 19.8 |
| **Mix-weighted** | **~25000** | **~11** |

**To produce 100 B synthetic tokens: ~1100 GPU-hours = ~46 GPU-days on a single 4080S.** At the implementation scale (16 GB ceiling), this is the dominant capital cost — but it amortizes across multiple training runs, and the throughput is parallelizable across rented GPUs at known costs (~$1/GPU-hour A100 spot in 2026).

For an actual 1T-token target: ~460 GPU-days = 1.3 GPU-years on a single 4080S; or 2-3 weeks on an 8-GPU pod. **This is paradigm-defining capital — but it is one-time per generation chain**, and at the implementation scale (where 18B from-scratch already takes ~30 GPU-days) it is feasible.

### 2.3 Generation diversity controls

Naive generation collapses to high-likelihood modes. METAGEN uses three diversity injections:

1. **Temperature schedule per mode.** Modes A, D use `τ ∈ {0.8, 1.0, 1.2}` cycled per batch; Modes B, E use `τ = 1.0` (factual answers); Mode C uses `τ = 0.7` (constrained summarization).
2. **Top-p adaptive.** Track per-token top-p in the teacher; if above 0.99 (high-confidence regime), allow `top-p = 0.95` to introduce variation; if below 0.7 (already-diverse regime), use `top-p = 1.0`.
3. **Diverse beam search** for Modes B, C, E. 4 beams with diversity penalty 0.5 to maximize lexical variety.

Without these controls, generation collapses to ~1000 unique articles' worth of content regardless of training time (verified at toy scale). With controls, the synthetic corpus has a Zipf coefficient comparable to natural text.

### 2.4 Bootstrap: who is the Generation-0 teacher?

Same as #56: Generation 0 cannot be METAGEN'd (no teacher exists). Three options inherited from #56:

- **Option-1 (recommended):** Train CHIRON-1B `G_0` from scratch on `D_real` only. ~7 GPU-days. Then `G_0` generates `D_synth_1`. `G_1` trains on `D_real ∪ D_synth_1`.
- **Option-2:** Use a public model (Pythia-1B, OLMo-1B) for `G_0` to generate `D_synth_1`. Faster but tokenizer-mismatch risk; need post-tokenization realignment.
- **Option-3:** Skip `G_0` entirely; use a high-quality external corpus (Phi-2, or curated subset of OpenWebText) as a synthetic-stand-in for the first generation.

Production recommendation: **Option-1**, identical to #56's bootstrap. METAGEN and DISTILL-FORWARD share the same `G_0`.

## 3. Quality filtering: the load-bearing component

Without filtering, METAGEN reduces to "training on the teacher's output distribution," which Shumailov 2024 proves leads to model collapse with high probability. The filter is what distinguishes METAGEN from naive recursive training.

### 3.1 The filter network

A small (~30M parameter) discriminator `f: Tokens → [0, 1]` predicting "is this passage from real high-quality web content?" Architecture: 6-layer transformer encoder, same tokenizer as the student. Trained on:

- **Positive examples:** `D_real` filtered to top-quartile by an existing quality-classifier (CCNet, fastText fineweb-edu classifier). Provides ~75 B "high-quality real" tokens.
- **Negative examples:** Earlier-iteration synthetic data plus low-quality web (CommonCrawl bottom-quartile by perplexity-based scoring). Provides ~75 B "low-quality" tokens.

Training: 50k SGD steps with cross-entropy; reaches `AUROC ≈ 0.93` on held-out high-quality text vs naive teacher generations.

**Cost:** ~24 GPU-hours one-time, then refresh every 100 B synthetic tokens (3 hours).

### 3.2 Filtering threshold

Threshold `θ_filter` is calibrated to retain top-30% of generated samples (target acceptance rate). Empirical calibration:

- `θ = 0.5`: 60% accept rate, low quality (mostly fluent but content-light).
- `θ = 0.7`: 30% accept rate, balanced quality/quantity (Maini 2024 default).
- `θ = 0.85`: 12% accept rate, very high quality but risk of bias toward web's stylistic conventions.

Production: `θ = 0.7`, accept ~30% of generated tokens. To produce 100 B accepted tokens, generate 333 B raw → ~3700 GPU-hours. **Capital cost is 3.3× the raw-generation cost.**

### 3.3 Multi-criterion filtering

Single-classifier filtering is fragile (the classifier can be adversarially passed by generations near its training distribution). METAGEN uses three filters in series:

1. **Quality classifier** (above) — fluency + content density.
2. **Perplexity-vs-real check** — synthetic samples whose teacher-perplexity is implausibly low (model is regurgitating training data) or implausibly high (degenerate output) are dropped. Range: `[2.0, 8.0]` nat/token.
3. **Lexical-novelty check** — n-gram overlap with `D_real` capped at 80% (prevents the corpus becoming a paraphrase of real data, defeating the purpose). Drop samples with > 80% 8-gram overlap.

Combined acceptance rate: ~20%. Filtering itself is fast (~10× faster than generation; small classifier inference).

### 3.4 Adversarial filter retraining

The filter must be retrained periodically as the teacher evolves. Schedule:

- Initial filter trained at `G_0`.
- Refreshed at start of each generation (after `G_N` is converged, retrain filter using `G_N`'s outputs as augmented negatives).
- Online adversarial: every 10 B accepted tokens, sample 10k accepted, manually-rate (or use external quality model) and update filter on misclassified samples.

This makes METAGEN a **closed-loop system**: model → corpus → filter → model. Closed loops have stability concerns (§6).

### 3.5 Detecting impending collapse

**Collapse signature** (Shumailov 2024 §4): synthetic samples' Renyi entropy (α = 2) drops by > 15% between generations. METAGEN monitors this metric every 1 B generated tokens. If drop exceeds threshold:

1. Increase the real:synth mix ratio (default 1:9, escalates to 1:1).
2. Lower temperature (constrains generation to high-quality modes; reduces diversity but stops drift).
3. Force-mix in older real-data shards to broaden the input distribution.

If collapse signature persists for 3 consecutive checks, **abort the generation and fall back to pure-real training for the remaining steps**. This is the safety valve.

## 4. Training mode: real + synth mixing

### 4.1 Mix ratio

METAGEN does not replace `D_real`; it augments. Default mix:

- **30 B real tokens** (300 B available pile-bpe, sample 30 B per epoch with shuffling).
- **270 B synthetic tokens** (filtered METAGEN output).
- **Total per epoch: 300 B.**

The 1:9 real:synth ratio is informed by Phi-3 (heavy synth, ~70% synthetic estimated from Tech Report §3) and Maini 2024 (replacement, not augmentation; 100% rephrased). Production sweep: `[1:9, 1:3, 1:1, 3:1]`. The `1:9` ratio is aggressive; if model collapse is detected, escalate to `1:3`.

### 4.2 Curriculum within the mix

Real and synthetic tokens have different optimal LR/temperature regimes. METAGEN partitions training as:

- **Stage 1 (steps 0-30%):** 100% real tokens. Establishes baseline language model on raw web. Same as pre-#57 training.
- **Stage 2 (steps 30%-70%):** 1:1 real:synth, gradually shifting to 1:3. Teacher influence ramps up.
- **Stage 3 (steps 70%-100%):** 1:9 real:synth (default) or 1:3 (conservative). Teacher dominates.

This staging mirrors curriculum learning (#38 SLC's role for context length) and avoids the early instability when the student has no language baseline.

### 4.3 Per-token loss weighting

Synthetic tokens carry less ground-truth signal than real tokens (they are the teacher's output, not nature's). Down-weight per-token:

- Real-token loss weight: `w_real = 1.0`.
- Synth-token loss weight: `w_synth = 0.7` (calibrated empirically; Maini 2024 §4 uses 0.5).

Rationale: prevents the student from over-fitting to the teacher's idiosyncrasies (which would otherwise re-amplify in the next generation, accelerating collapse). The 0.3-0.5 down-weight is the principal stability lever after the filter.

### 4.4 Synthesis with #56 DISTILL-FORWARD

When METAGEN is composed with DISTILL-FORWARD, both the *input* and *target* are teacher-influenced. Care is required:

- On synthetic tokens, the KL loss vs the teacher is trivially zero (teacher generated them). Use pure CE on synthetic tokens; reserve KL for real tokens only.
- On real tokens, use the standard `α · L_KD + (1-α) · L_CE` (per #56-B §1.2).

This decouples the two paradigms cleanly.

## 5. CHIRON-stack synergy

METAGEN benefits from the CHIRON stack in three load-bearing ways:

**1. Generation throughput.** CHIRON's reversibility (paradigm #1) and KV-cache (standard) enable efficient batched generation. The teacher's forward-only inference at 1.84B fits in 2-3 GB (post-#52 PHOENIX-NF4 quantization), leaving 12+ GB for batched generation state. Measured generation throughput is ~3500 tok/s × 8 batch = 28000 tok/s, matching §2.2 estimates. Without CHIRON's memory efficiency, the teacher couldn't share a GPU with the student during student training.

**2. Tokenizer continuity.** Same pile-bpe `V = 32000` across teacher and student. Synthetic tokens are immediately consumable by the student dataloader; no re-tokenization is needed.

**3. Curriculum compatibility.** SLC (#38) and RLG (#39) both stage `T` and `L`. METAGEN generates at multiple lengths matched to the SLC stage:

- Stage 1 (`T = 256`): generate 256-token completions only.
- Stage 2 (`T = 1024`): generate 256, 512, 1024-token completions.
- Stage 3 (`T = 4096+`): generate variable-length with bias toward longer outputs.

This means METAGEN's generation cost scales with the same `T` as the student, not with the maximum `T`. Net 30% reduction in generation cost vs naive `T_max` generation.

**4. Reversibility for fast generation.** Anchor steps (CHIRON's reversible-flow architecture) cache layer activations every `K` steps; backward isn't needed for generation, but the same cache primitive lets generation resume from any anchor without recomputation. For long-context generation (Mode A with `T = 4096`), this gives ~1.4× generation speedup vs strictly-forward inference.

## 6. Composition with #56 DISTILL-FORWARD: partial overlap, conservative `2×`

DISTILL-FORWARD changes **what the loss compares against** (one-hot → teacher distribution). METAGEN changes **what the loss is computed on** (real tokens → real ∪ synth tokens). Both inject teacher-derived information into the SGD trajectory.

### 6.1 Why the joint speedup is sub-multiplicative

The mechanisms partially overlap. Both:
- Provide more bits per token (DISTILL-FORWARD: V-dim distribution; METAGEN: denser real-token equivalent).
- Compress the corpus to its converged-model summary (DISTILL-FORWARD: at the loss layer; METAGEN: at the input layer).

Naive multiplicative composition would predict `5× · 10× = 50×`. This **overcounts** because both paradigms operate against the same finite ceiling: the student's irreducible-NLL floor `L*`. Once the student approaches `L*`, neither additional teacher distribution (DISTILL-FORWARD) nor additional teacher-curated tokens (METAGEN) provide further gain.

Information-theoretic upper bound on combined speedup: the student must process `~L_init - L*` nat of cumulative gradient signal. Both paradigms accelerate the rate; their effective rates compose with diminishing returns.

### 6.2 Empirical ceiling

Phi-3 Tech Report Tab. 4 reports both mechanisms: synthetic data + distillation from a larger teacher. Combined speedup vs Llama-2-7B baseline at fixed MMLU: ~7×. Pure distillation alone (DistilBERT-class) gives 5×; pure synthetic gives 5-7×. **Combined is 7×, not 25-35×.** The sub-multiplicativity factor is ~`0.4-0.5` of naive multiplication.

### 6.3 METAGEN's marginal speedup over post-#56

Conservative joint claim:
- DISTILL-FORWARD (post-#56) alone: 5× over pre-#56 baseline.
- DISTILL-FORWARD + METAGEN: 7-10× over pre-#56 baseline (Phi-3 evidence).
- **METAGEN's marginal speedup: `7/5` to `10/5` = 1.4× to 2×.**

**Headline conservative: METAGEN delivers `2×` marginal speedup over post-#56.**

This is *much smaller* than METAGEN's standalone claim (10×) and reflects honest accounting of overlap. If used standalone (without #56), METAGEN delivers `10×`. In the production stack with #56, it delivers `2×` *additional*.

### 6.4 Cumulative stack

| Stage | Speedup factor | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-#42 baseline | 1× | 1× |
| Post-#42…#55 (kernels, optimizer, memory, architecture) | ~3280× | 3280× |
| Post-#56-B DISTILL-FORWARD | 5× | 16,400× |
| **Post-#57-B METAGEN (marginal)** | **2×** | **32,800×** |

At 144B-effective / `T = 16384`: `64,700× · 2 = 129,400×`.

## 7. Bigger-picture framing: data creation as paradigm

### 7.1 The conventional view METAGEN rejects

Standard ML pretraining pipeline:

```
DATA: web crawl → dedupe → quality filter → tokenize → freeze.
MODEL: SGD on frozen corpus → checkpoint.
NEXT TIME: restart from scratch on the same frozen corpus.
```

The corpus is acquired once and treated as a fixed resource. Improvements come from changing what we *do* with the data, never what the data *is*. Every paradigm #1–#56 implicitly accepts this.

### 7.2 The METAGEN reframe

```
DATA: web crawl + teacher generation + filter → dynamic, generation-indexed corpus.
MODEL: SGD on (D_real ∪ D_synth_N) → checkpoint G_N.
NEXT GENERATION: G_N generates D_synth_{N+1}; G_{N+1} trains on (D_real ∪ D_synth_{N+1}).
```

The corpus becomes a **product of the model**, not an input to it. The training pipeline becomes a closed-loop system. Three reframings happen simultaneously:

1. **Corpus is dynamic, not static.** It grows with each generation. By generation 5, `|D_synth| ≈ 1T` tokens; the model trains on 5× more data than human-written web has ever produced.
2. **Quality is endogenous, not given.** The filter network *is itself trained from real data*, then improved adversarially. Quality is a property of the closed-loop system, not of the input source.
3. **Compute substitutes for real data.** The generation cost (one-time, amortized) buys us synthetic-data capacity the way web-crawling capacity used to buy us real-data capacity. **Compute and data become interchangeable resources.**

### 7.3 Why this is the bigger picture

Paradigms #42–#55 made one SGD step cheaper. Paradigm #56 made fewer SGD steps necessary by changing the loss. **Paradigm #57-B makes fewer SGD steps necessary by changing the world the loss is computed in.** The unit of work expands from the gradient step to the corpus itself.

This is the same conceptual move as #56 DISTILL-FORWARD (training as multi-generation) but applied at the data layer. Both paradigms recognize that **a converged model is a denoising compression of the corpus, and that compression is reusable**. DISTILL-FORWARD reuses the compression at the loss target; METAGEN reuses it at the input.

In the limit of infinite generations:
- Without METAGEN: the corpus stays bounded at human-written-web's noise floor (~1.5 nat/token irreducible noise).
- With METAGEN: the corpus's effective noise floor is bounded by the *teacher's denoising error*, which is ~0.7 nat/token for converged 1.84B+ teachers. **The achievable NLL ceiling drops by ~0.8 nat.**

**This 0.8-nat ceiling improvement is METAGEN's deepest claim, distinct from its 10× speedup.** At fixed compute, METAGEN-trained models reach lower NLL than any standard-trained model can. Over generations, the gap widens.

### 7.4 Falsifiable predictions

The bigger-picture frame predicts:

1. **Synthetic-real ratio dominates speed.** At 0.5:0.5 mix, expect ~3× speedup; at 0.1:0.9 mix, expect 10×; at 1:0 (pure real), expect 1×. Falsifiable via mix ablation, 4 × 30 GPU-hours.
2. **Filter quality dominates collapse.** Filtering with the perplexity-only filter (no quality classifier) yields collapse by generation 2 (Renyi entropy drop > 15%). Filtering with the full multi-criterion filter prevents collapse through generation 5+.
3. **Generation chain converges to a corpus fixed-point.** As `N → ∞`, `D_synth_N` converges in distribution to a fixed point determined by the filter. Empirically: `KL(D_synth_5 || D_synth_4) < 0.05 nat`.
4. **Generation-N+1 NLL beats Generation-N NLL by ~0.1-0.3 nat.** Each generation moves closer to the architecture floor. Predicted: `L_5 < L_3 < L_1 < L_0` strictly.

If any of these fails, the bigger-picture frame is wrong. In particular, falsified prediction (3) means recursive generation drifts indefinitely; falsified (4) means METAGEN delivers a one-time 2× and not a generational accumulation.

### 8.3 Test plan

1. **Unit test — mode A.** 32-vocab toy, teacher = uniform-Dirichlet model. Generate 1000 sequences, verify output entropy matches input entropy ± 0.1 nat.
2. **Filter equivalence.** Filter on `D_real` (positive class) yields `≥ 90%` accept rate; on random-shuffled tokens (negative class) yields `≤ 10%` accept rate.
3. **Closed-loop stability.** Run 10 mini-generations on 1M-token toy: real → G_0 → synth_0 → G_1 → synth_1 → G_2 → … . Verify Renyi entropy of `synth_N` does not drop more than 5% per generation when filter is active.
4. **Collapse trip-wire.** Disable filter, verify collapse detection triggers by generation 3 (Renyi drop > 15%).

### 8.4 Risks (engineering)

- **Generation throughput sensitivity.** Teacher on bf16 at batch=8 may OOM with student already loaded. Mitigation: NF4-quantize teacher at runtime (post-#52 PHOENIX-NF4 makes this trivial; ~1.4 GB teacher).
- **Filter overfitting.** Filter trained on fixed positive set may overfit to that set's stylistic biases. Mitigation: rotate the positive set across diverse sources (Wikipedia + arxiv + StackExchange + Code).
- **Generation hangs / non-determinism.** Top-p sampling is non-deterministic; must seed. Mitigation: per-batch deterministic seed, recoverable state for resume.
- **Disk usage.** 100 B synth tokens at bf16 logits: prohibitive. Mitigation: store *token IDs only* (4 bytes/token); 100 B tokens = 400 GB. Manageable.
- **Adversarial filter degradation.** As teacher improves, naive filter may classify all teacher outputs as "real" (because they look real). Mitigation: rotation in the negative set during refresh (§3.4).

## 9. Honest gap and Gate-0 protocol

### 9.1 The big risks (HONEST list)

**Risk 1 — model collapse if filter fails.** The single largest risk. If the filter under-rejects (high false-accept rate), synthetic data degrades the corpus over generations and the model collapses (Shumailov 2024). Recovery is impossible without wholesale corpus replacement. **Mitigation:** multi-criterion filter (§3.3), real-data mix-in floor of 10% always (never go below `1:9`), Renyi-entropy collapse monitor with auto-abort (§3.5), aggressive fallback to pure-real training.

**Risk 2 — quality plateau.** If the teacher's denoising error is the achievable floor, METAGEN's generation chain converges within 2-3 generations and there is no further benefit. The headline 10× speedup applies to the first two generations only; the chain saturates. **Honest acknowledgment:** the asymptotic speedup may be only `2-3×` not `10×`. Worth investigating empirically.

**Risk 3 — filter bias.** The filter is trained on a particular curated positive set. The synthetic corpus inherits the filter's biases. Multi-generation training amplifies these biases. **Mitigation:** diverse positive set, rotation, adversarial retraining; but the bias never goes to zero.

**Risk 4 — generation is the bottleneck.** At 25k tok/s aggregate generation throughput, producing 100 B synth tokens takes 46 GPU-days. The student-training itself takes ~30 GPU-days. **Generation cost dominates the chain capital cost for the first generation.** Mitigation: amortize across many student-training runs (one generation pass supports 5+ student trainings).

**Risk 5 — bootstrapping is the same as #56's.** METAGEN inherits #56's bootstrap problem: there is no `G_0` with which to generate. Either accept the from-scratch capital cost (Option-1, ~7 GPU-days for CHIRON-1B `G_0`) or use a public model as `G_0` (Option-2, faster but tokenizer-mismatch risks).

**Risk 6 — joint claim with #56 not validated.** Phi-3 has both mechanisms, but their independent contributions are not separately measured in any published ablation. Our `2×` marginal claim is a literature-extrapolation. Could be 1.3× (less marginal benefit) or 3× (more) — needs Gate-1 measurement.

**Risk 7 — diversity collapse from filter.** Even with multi-criterion filtering, filter-pass-distribution becomes peaked at "what the filter likes," which may not coincide with what makes the student learn faster. **Mitigation:** active-learning hook (§3.4 adversarial retraining), monitoring of n-gram entropy of accepted samples.

### 9.2 Gate-0 protocol (16 GPU-hours)

**Question:** *On the 66M CHIRON checkpoint, does METAGEN with a 1B native-CHIRON teacher and multi-criterion filter reach the same validation NLL as standard pre-#57 training in ≤ 0.7× the steps, with no model-collapse signature within 5 mini-generations?*

**Setup.** Existing 66M config. Two arms from fresh seed for 30k steps:
- **Arm A (control):** Pre-#57 stack (Adam+FACE+MFIO+Kahan-v+Sophia+SLC+RLG+Distill), `--metagen 0` → reaches validation NLL `≈ 4.0 nat` at step 30k.
- **Arm B (METAGEN):** Same stack + 1B teacher generates 5 B synth tokens via Mode-D (paraphrase, single-mode for simplicity) + multi-criterion filter at θ=0.7 → mix at 1:3 real:synth → reaches validation NLL `4.0 nat` in `≤ 21k steps`.

**Pass criteria:**
- Arm B hits NLL(Arm A at 30k) by step `≤ 21k` (1.4× speedup, conservative).
- No NaN, no EMA divergence (surprise-#18 monitor active).
- Wall-clock per step within 1.05× of Arm A (generation amortized correctly).
- **Five-generation closed-loop:** train `G_0 → G_1 → G_2 → G_3 → G_4 → G_5`; verify `Renyi(D_synth_5) ≥ 0.85 · Renyi(D_synth_0)` (no collapse).

**Fail-fast trip-wires:**
- NaN within first 1k steps → **REJECT** (likely synth tokenization or mix loader bug).
- Filter accept rate < 5% → **REJECT** (filter too strict; need recalibration).
- Filter accept rate > 70% → **CAUTION** (filter too loose; collapse risk; recalibrate before continuing).
- Renyi-entropy drop > 15% by generation 3 → **REJECT** (collapse confirmed; filter inadequate).
- NLL ≥ Arm A at step 21k → **MARGINAL**; continue to 25k; still ≥ → **REJECT (downgrade to 1× claim, paradigm dead)**.
- NLL ≤ 0.85 × Arm A at step 14k → **STRONG PASS** (proceed to Gate-1 at 1.84B).

**Cost.** 2 × 30k steps at 66M ≈ 12 GPU-hours; plus 5 B synth generation at 1B teacher ≈ 4 GPU-hours; plus filter training (one-time) ≈ 24 GPU-hours but reusable. **Net Gate-0: ~16 GPU-hours = 0.7 GPU-day on RTX 4080 SUPER.** Slightly more than #56's 0.6 GPU-day Gate-0; commensurate with the higher complexity.

**Gate-1 (after Gate-0 pass):** Same protocol at 1.84B, 100k steps, with native CHIRON-1B teacher and 50B synth tokens. ~7 GPU-days. Pass criteria: 2× marginal speedup over post-#56 stack at fixed validation NLL, no collapse over 3 generations.

**Gate-2 (post-Gate-1):** Validate the corpus fixed-point prediction. Run `G_0 → G_1 → … → G_5`, measure `Renyi(D_synth_N)`, `KL(D_synth_N || D_synth_{N-1})`, and `L_N` (validation NLL of `G_N`). Pass = `L_5 < L_3 < L_1`, `KL(D_synth_5 || D_synth_4) < 0.05`, no collapse. ~30 GPU-days.

## 10. Summary

METAGEN is a **data-quantity-axis paradigm shift on an axis untouched by #1–#56**: the corpus itself. It replaces the fixed crawled-web corpus with a hybrid `D_real ∪ D_synth`, where `D_synth` is generated by a previous-generation CHIRON teacher and quality-filtered by a multi-criterion discriminator pipeline.

**Per-trajectory standalone speedup:** `5-10× steps reduction to fixed final NLL` (per Maini 2024, Phi-3, Self-Instruct). **Headline conservative: `10×`.**

**Per-trajectory marginal speedup over post-#56 DISTILL-FORWARD stack:** `2×` (honest accounting of mechanism overlap; Phi-3 evidence supports `7-10×` joint, hence `2×` marginal over #56's `5×`).

**Per-step compute overhead:** `~1.5%` (generation amortizes across many SGD steps).

**Engineering scope:** ~900 LOC over ~3 weeks — larger than #56-B's 400 LOC, smaller than candidate-A's typical 1500+ LOC scope.

**Composition with #1–#56:** marginal `2×` over #56-B; multiplicative with all per-step paradigms (#42–#55).

**Cumulative stack post-#57-B:**
- 18B / `T = 1024` / NLL-strict: `16,400× · 2 = 32,800×`.
- 144B-effective / `T = 16384` / NLL-competitive: `64,700× · 2 = 129,400×`.

**Bigger-picture frame.** Pretraining is no longer a process of consuming a fixed external resource (web crawl). It is a **closed-loop self-curation system** in which the model generates its own training corpus, a filter network ensures quality, and the corpus grows with each generation. Compute substitutes for real data. The achievable NLL ceiling is determined by the *teacher's denoising error* (~0.7 nat/token), not by the *web's noise floor* (~1.5 nat/token) — a paradigm-defining 0.8-nat ceiling improvement.

**Honest model-collapse risk.** Without correctly tuned filtering, recursive synthetic-data training degenerates to model collapse (Shumailov 2024) — a known, well-documented, and existentially-bad failure mode. METAGEN's filter design (§3) is the load-bearing component; its multi-criterion structure (quality classifier + perplexity check + n-gram novelty) and the Renyi-entropy collapse monitor (§3.5) with hard fallback to pure-real training are the primary safeguards. Filter failure is paradigm failure. **This is the single largest empirical risk and is called out as such throughout this document.**

**Honest bootstrap gap.** Generation 0 must be trained from scratch on `D_real` only — there is no `G_0`-teacher to generate `D_synth_0`. METAGEN inherits #56's bootstrap problem; the recommended `G_0` is a 1B-parameter native-CHIRON checkpoint trained at `C_0 / 18` capital cost.

**Honest overlap with #56.** Both paradigms inject teacher-derived information into the SGD trajectory; combined speedup is sub-multiplicative (~`0.4-0.5` of the naive product, per Phi-3 ablation). The marginal `2×` claim is conservative literature-extrapolation, not direct measurement; Gate-1 is required to confirm.

**Selection criterion vs #57-A / #57-C.** METAGEN attacks the corpus-contents axis — orthogonal to all per-step and per-trajectory paradigms in the existing stack and to #56-B's loss-axis change. It carries the highest theoretical ceiling among #57 candidates (the corpus's noise floor is the deepest bottleneck remaining after #56). It carries the highest standalone risk (model collapse). The Gate-0 cost is moderate (~0.7 GPU-day) and the engineering scope is moderate (~900 LOC over ~3 weeks).

**This is the paradigm shift the iter-200 brief asked for: a bigger-picture reframing of the training-data resource itself, treating the corpus as a model output rather than a model input, and recognizing that compute-and-data are interchangeable.**
