## Training-loss EMA + per-step variance

Per-step training losses bounce dramatically depending on which
denoiser was rolled:

- R-denoiser steps: training loss ~1.4-2.0 (same-position prediction
  is trivial — model can copy 85% non-masked positions and partial-
  predict at masked positions using local context).
- S-denoiser steps: training loss ~4-7 (standard causal LM, at 5k
  steps the loss is consistent with ~1500-step pure-S training).
- X-denoiser steps: training loss ~5-6 (heavier masking but easier
  task than causal LM since same-position has full bidirectional
  context within the kept positions).

EMA at step 4983 = 4.5609 (vs U0 EMA at same step ~4.04). The mixture
training EMA is dominated by S-step loss because R-step loss is so
low; net EMA is ~0.5 nat higher than pure-S training EMA.

Training was STABLE across all 5000 steps — no NaN, no gradient
explosion, no divergence events of the magnitude LayerDrop showed
(LayerDrop had train loss spikes of 9.6, 13.3; U1 had max ~6.4 which
is the X-denoiser cold-region noise). The mechanism is mathematically
well-behaved; it's just **not learning causal LM well**.

## Three plausible explanations

1. **Same-position prediction objective is structurally easier than
   causal LM** — and the model's gradient signal during R/X steps
   tells it that prediction is much easier than causal LM suggests.
   With 2/3 of training steps reinforcing "easy" task representations
   and only 1/3 reinforcing the harder causal LM, the model converges
   to a representation that's good at easy denoising and bad at hard
   causal LM. The "shared weight set" assumption (a single transformer
   handles all three denoisers via shared parameters) doesn't pay off
   at this scale and architecture. This is the most plausible cause.

2. **CHIRON's symplectic update is sub-optimal for same-position
   prediction.** The `(p, q) → (p + shear(q), reln(q))` structure
   was designed for next-token prediction where each layer
   progressively refines a representation that will be decoded by
   the readout at the next token's position. Same-position prediction
   is conceptually a "decoder predicts input at same position via
   context"; the symplectic update's directional information flow may
   not be well-suited. (This is similar to LayerDrop's symplectic-
   incompatibility finding but for a different reason.)

3. **5k is too early for UL2's data-efficiency benefit to overcome
   the mixture-training penalty.** UL2's literature gains (-0.10 to
   -0.20 nat) typically materialize at 100k+ steps. At 5k single-
   seed, the model is still in the "learning representations" regime
   where mixture training is pure cost. But spec §3.2 §3.4 are firm:
   the +0.05 nat 5k pilot bar exists explicitly to avoid the "maybe
   at 30k+" rationalization. Phase-3 P6 prohibits silent re-targeting
   to longer training horizons.

The +2.33 nat magnitude is too large to be explained by (3) alone.
Literature gains at 5k are typically modest (R-only denoisers can
show -0.05 to +0.05 nat at 5k); a +2.33 nat regression is symptomatic
of (1) being the dominant mechanism. Hypothesis (1) is the cleanest
read.

This is consistent with the broader observation from this Phase-3
session: **CHIRON 1B's symplectic architecture appears to resist
training-objective and training-discipline changes more than the
optimizer-side and attention-side changes that previously shipped
(regstack Phase 2: Z-loss + QK-Norm).** Both LayerDrop (depth-noise)
and UL2 (objective-mixture) failed at 5k by significant margins
despite paper-validated effects at the corresponding scale on
standard residual transformers.

## Honest publication per Phase-3 P6

Negative results are first-class outputs of the Phase-3 program. This
is the second arc (after LayerDrop) closed FAIL on 2026-05-23. The
combined arc lessons:

- **Architecture-side regularization** (LayerDrop depth-noise) FAILed
  due to CHIRON symplectic-update incompatibility (hard-drop on
  momentum accumulator).
- **Objective-side regularization** (UL2 mixture-of-denoisers)
  FAILed at much larger magnitude due to (likely) same-position
  prediction being too-easy a task for the symplectic update's
  representation pattern, causing the shared-weight assumption to
  collapse causal-LM performance.

Going forward, regularization candidates for CHIRON should be either:
(a) within the optimizer-side / wall-time / attention-side class
that previously shipped (this is the territory where the existing
production stack lives), OR (b) accompanied by a theoretical argument
for why the mechanism interacts cleanly with the symplectic
`(p, q) → (p + shear(q), reln(q))` update structure rather than
fighting it. Pure ports from standard-residual-transformer literature
have now failed twice in a row.

**Next arc candidate (open):** the user may want to consider either:
- Returning to optimizer/wall-time arc territory (the iter-N
  pre-ralph-loop research line).
- Designing a regularizer specifically for symplectic transformers
  (which would be a research contribution, not just an arc).
- Accepting the regstack Phase 2 ship as the stable production
  flagship and declaring the Phase-3 regularization sub-program
  closed (LayerDrop FAIL, UL2 FAIL, MTP earlier NEGATIVE — three
  arcs in a row, all closed honestly).
